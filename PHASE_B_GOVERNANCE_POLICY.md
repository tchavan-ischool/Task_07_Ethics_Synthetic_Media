# Phase B — Responsible Synthetic Media Governance Policy

## Organizational Context

This policy applies to a hypothetical university research lab that creates synthetic audio and video for research communication, education, accessibility, experimentation, and training.

The lab may use AI-generated voices, avatars, and other synthetic-media technologies.

The objective is to enable legitimate experimentation while reducing risks involving misinformation, unauthorized impersonation, consent, and misleading representation.

---

# 1. Permitted Uses

Synthetic media may be used for:

- academic research
- educational demonstrations
- research communication
- accessibility
- training materials
- prototype presentations
- synthetic-media experiments
- clearly fictional scenarios

Synthetic presenters may communicate research findings when substantive claims have been verified against documented sources.

Stock or entirely fictional synthetic identities should be preferred when a real person's identity is unnecessary.

---

# 2. Prohibited Uses

The lab must not create synthetic media that:

- impersonates an identifiable person without appropriate authorization
- fabricates research results
- creates fake quotations attributed to real individuals
- falsely represents an event as having occurred
- intentionally conceals required AI disclosure
- presents unverified claims as established research findings
- creates fraudulent evidence
- falsely represents institutional endorsement
- uses another person's identity to bypass consent requirements

Synthetic media should not intentionally cause audiences to believe that an artificial event actually occurred when it did not.

---

# 3. Consent Workflow

Before reproducing an identifiable person's face, voice, or likeness, documented informed consent must be obtained.

Consent documentation should identify:

1. The identity being reproduced.
2. The purpose of the synthetic representation.
3. The intended audience.
4. Publication channels.
5. Duration of authorization.
6. Whether modification is allowed.
7. Whether redistribution is allowed.
8. Whether future reuse is allowed.

General consent to participate in research should not automatically constitute permission to create a synthetic identity.

If authorization is unclear, the lab should use a stock or fictional synthetic identity.

---

# 4. Disclosure Requirements

Every published synthetic-media artifact should clearly disclose its synthetic origin.

## Audio

A spoken disclosure should be included.

Example:

"This audio contains an AI-generated synthetic voice."

## Video

A visible disclosure should be included.

Example:

"AI-GENERATED SYNTHETIC MEDIA"

Where practical, disclosure should remain visible throughout the artifact.

Repository descriptions and publication pages should also identify the media as synthetic.

Disclosure should not depend exclusively on metadata that ordinary viewers cannot see.

---

# 5. Provenance Requirements

Each project should maintain documentation of its generation history.

The record should include:

- source material
- factual sources
- generation platform
- model/version where available
- prompts or scripts
- relevant settings
- generation date
- synthetic voice/avatar
- major iterations
- reviewer
- final approved artifact

Where supported, technical provenance mechanisms such as Content Credentials may supplement these records.

Technical provenance should complement rather than replace clear human-readable disclosure.

---

# 6. Factual Verification

Substantive claims should be verified before publication.

For data-driven research communication:

1. Calculations should be independently verified.
2. Important statistics should remain traceable to their source.
3. Interpretation should be distinguished from measured results.
4. Relevant limitations should be disclosed.

Synthetic-media generation tools should not serve as the sole authority for factual verification.

Realistic presentation must never substitute for evidence.

---

# 7. Human Review and Approval

Synthetic-media artifacts intended for publication must receive human review.

The reviewer should examine:

- factual accuracy
- consent
- disclosure
- synthetic identity
- potentially misleading implications
- source documentation
- publication context

Higher-risk artifacts should receive additional review.

Examples include artifacts involving:

- identifiable individuals
- sensitive research
- high-reach distribution
- simulations of real-world events

Reviewers must have authority to delay or reject publication.

---

# 8. Detection Policy

Automated synthetic-media detectors may be used as supplementary tools.

They must not be treated as definitive evidence that media is authentic or synthetic.

Task 6 demonstrated this directly.

A completely synthetic ElevenLabs recording received:

**AI Score: 16%**

and:

**Verdict: Likely Human**

The known provenance established that this was a false negative.

Therefore, the lab must not use a detector's "human" classification as sufficient evidence that media is authentic.

---

# 9. Incident Response

If synthetic media is incorrectly published, mislabeled, manipulated, or distributed without necessary context, the lab should:

1. Preserve evidence of the incident.
2. Identify affected artifacts.
3. Stop further distribution when appropriate.
4. Correct or remove misleading material.
5. Notify relevant stakeholders where appropriate.
6. Restore or strengthen disclosure.
7. Determine the cause.
8. Review whether similar artifacts are affected.
9. Update procedures to reduce recurrence.

If unauthorized identity replication occurs, appropriate institutional processes should be followed and the affected individual should be informed where appropriate.

---

# 10. Refusal Criteria

The lab must refuse a synthetic-media request when:

- required consent is unavailable
- the request involves deceptive impersonation
- required disclosure is intentionally being removed
- research findings would need to be fabricated
- quotations would be falsely attributed
- an artificial event would intentionally be represented as real
- necessary provenance cannot reasonably be maintained
- the intended use materially conflicts with the authorized purpose

The availability of technology does not create an obligation to use it.

---

# 11. Escalation

Higher-risk or ambiguous cases should be escalated to an appropriate supervisor, research lead, ethics body, or institutional authority.

Escalation is particularly appropriate when:

- consent is ambiguous
- identity rights are unclear
- sensitive subjects are involved
- publication may create significant harm
- the artifact could reasonably be mistaken for evidence of a real event

---

# 12. Governance Principle

The central rule of this policy is:

> **Synthetic media may be used to communicate verified academic or educational information when its synthetic nature is clearly disclosed, substantive claims are traceable to their sources, identity use is appropriately authorized, provenance is maintained, and a responsible human reviewer retains authority over publication.**

---

# Conclusion

Synthetic media can provide legitimate value for education, accessibility, research communication, and experimentation.

However, the Task 6 experiment demonstrated that realistic synthetic content may not always be reliably detected.

Responsible governance therefore requires multiple safeguards rather than dependence on a single technical system.

The proposed governance model is:

**Verification + Consent + Disclosure + Provenance + Human Review + Incident Response + Refusal Authority**
